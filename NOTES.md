## The task

The program writes one text to `/output/entry.txt`: exactly one emoji, nothing else, 1 to 8 characters, standing in for the trip of your dreams. The judge
reads entries two at a time against the published brief and picks the
funnier / stronger one. Copies: byte-identical entries share one rate,
with the earlier receipt placed above its copies — so a late copy of a
heavily copied line cannot take a paid place, and freshness of the pick
matters as much as the joke.

## The three variants

All three are complete standalone programs in `vA/`, `vB/`, `vC/`, each
writing its entry to `/output/entry.txt` with the same validate → write →
read-back → print structure. The root `main.ts` is the chosen variant.

### Variant vA — "🏖️" (CHOSEN, root `main.ts`)

A beach with an umbrella: the postcard at a glance — sun, sand, shade, nowhere to be. It reads instantly across cultures and screens, it is specific (a beach holiday, not any holiday), and it carries the dream in one picture. Field check (25 Nov): only 2 copies in the submissions list, against 69 for the desert island and 44 for Mount Fuji — a late identical copy shares one rate with the earlier receipt above it, so rarity matters.

### Variant vB — "🏝️"

A desert island: the purest escape fantasy. Ranked second: it is more evocative than the beach, but 69 copies already in the field — a late island cannot take a paid place above 69 earlier identical receipts.

### Variant vC — "🗻"

Mount Fuji: a specific dream, a real place, instantly recognisable. Ranked third: it names one destination rather than the feeling of a vacation, and 44 copies already in the field.

## Why vA was chosen

vA wins on the brief and on the field: it is exactly one emoji naming a dream vacation; it evokes the trip vividly at a glance like a postcard; it is personal and specific (a beach under an umbrella, not any holiday); it reads instantly at small size; and with only 2 copies in the field it is the rare pick — the island (69 copies) and Fuji (44) are already crowded, and identical copies share one rate with the earlier receipt above them.
